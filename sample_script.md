# The Rise of AI

## The Quiet Beginning

Decades before anyone called it artificial intelligence, mathematicians were already dreaming of thinking machines. Alan Turing asked a deceptively simple question: can a machine think? That question would echo across the next century and change everything.

## The First Wave

Early computers filled entire rooms. They were powerful for their time, solving equations that would take humans years. But they could only do what they were explicitly told. They had no ability to learn, no ability to adapt.

## The Long Winter

Progress stalled. Twice, funding dried up and the field nearly died. These became known as the AI winters. Researchers kept working, quietly, in labs around the world, convinced that a breakthrough was coming.

## The Neural Revolution

Everything changed when researchers revisited an old idea: the artificial neural network. Inspired by the human brain, these systems could learn patterns from raw data. Give them enough examples, and they would figure out the rules themselves.

## The Data Explosion

The internet gave AI something it had always lacked: scale. Billions of images, conversations, and documents became training material. The machines began to see, to understand language, and eventually to create.

## Where We Stand Today

Today, AI writes code, composes music, and holds conversations indistinguishable from a human's. We are only at the beginning. The question Alan Turing asked is no longer hypothetical. It has been answered — and the answer keeps evolving.
